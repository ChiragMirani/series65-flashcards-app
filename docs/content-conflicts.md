| Conflict or qualification | Treatment |
|---|---|
| NASAA's 2023 coordinated-exams presentation, slide 16, summarizes the prepaid-fee time test as greater than six months. The official form and the state financial-reporting model use an inclusive six-month boundary. | Follow [Form ADV Part 2A, Item 18.A–B and state note, as published by NASAA](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Part2andSched.02-2015.pdf#page=14) and [Model Rule 203(c)-1(a)](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/model-rule-investment-adviser-financial-reporting.pdf): the dollar amount must be exceeded, while exactly six months qualifies. The [presentation](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2023-Investment-Adviser-Coordinated-Exams.pdf#page=16) is not the authority for that boundary. |
| A quarterly fee can exceed the dollar threshold without meeting the substantial-prepayment time test. This does not establish that an adviser has no other financial-reporting duties. | Original state and federal examples specify payments for three months only and ask whether prepayment alone triggers the audit. Existing independent custody/net-worth duties remain separate. The ordinary established-firm balance sheet covers the most recent fiscal year; the form also identifies new-firm treatment and qualified-custodian/insurance-company exceptions. |
| The notes place brochure delivery at the initial sales presentation; the canonical state model ties delivery to contracting with the 48-hour/five-business-day alternatives. | Preserve the canonical state cards. The federal general rule instead requires delivery before or at contracting. An earlier solicitation-related obligation needs its own legal context; do not present one timing rule as universal. References: [NASAA model](https://www.nasaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/model-rule-investment-adviser-brochure.pdf), [federal rule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.204-3). |
| Some pasted age boundaries display 59 without the half-year. | Retain 59½. At age 60 the age-based exception applies, but age 59 alone does not establish it. [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc410). |
| A particular allocation is described as suitable based on limited information about an older, high-income client. Canonical section 26 requires knowing risk tolerance. | Retain the complete-profile requirement; do not teach a universal allocation by age or characterize stock downside as assuredly modest. [SEC investor guidance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation). |
| The notes describe investment income entering DNI broadly, then exclude reinvested capital gains. | Preserve the canonical distinction: taxable interest, dividends, and tax-exempt interest generally enter DNI; capital gains generally do not in the stated principal-retention situation. | A hedge explanation treats inflation risk as exclusive to fixed-income investments. | Do not import that exclusion. Teach the call as protection against a future repurchase-price increase; avoid claiming that equity owners cannot suffer purchasing-power loss. |
| Investor preferences are expressed as simultaneous maximum return and minimum risk. | Keep the canonical rational risk/return preference as a model assumption. New wording should hold one variable fixed when comparing the other and should not promise an attainable maximum-return/minimum-risk product. |
